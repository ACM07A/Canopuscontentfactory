# Breast Cancer Treatment in India: Pathology, Surgery, Chemo, Radiation, Costs, Hospitals & Doctors

**18–28 minute international-patient resource**

A breast-cancer diagnosis often arrives with several new decisions at once: whether more tests are needed, whether treatment should begin with surgery or medicines, and what the pathology means for the months ahead. You do not need to understand every abbreviation immediately. Begin with what the biopsy confirms, whether staging is complete and which result will determine the next step.

Breast cancer treatment is determined by much more than the words “breast cancer.” The treatment plan can depend on the tumour type, size, lymph-node involvement, whether disease has spread elsewhere, tumour grade, hormone-receptor status, HER2 status, other biomarkers, general health, prior treatment and patient preferences.

The US National Cancer Institute (NCI) explains that breast cancer stage and tumour factors are central to treatment planning and that many patients receive more than one type of treatment. Depending on the case, treatment may include breast surgery, lymph-node surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy and—in selected triple-negative settings—immunotherapy.

For an international patient, this means a hospital “package” should never be compared until you know what treatment sequence it assumes.

# Quick answer

NCI describes breast cancer treatment as stage- and biology-dependent. For stages I–III, surgery is common, but some tumours receive chemotherapy or targeted treatment before surgery. After surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, hormone therapy and/or targeted therapy may be used depending on pathology and tumour characteristics. Metastatic breast cancer has a different treatment goal and pathway.

NCI also explains that staging incorporates tumour and lymph-node information plus grade and biomarker status, including hormone-receptor/HER2 information.

Apollo Hospitals currently publishes approximately **₹1–2.5 lakh** for breast cancer surgery on its general breast-cancer-surgery procedure page. That is a **surgical episode reference**, not the cost of the entire breast-cancer journey. If chemotherapy, radiation, targeted therapy, reconstruction or extended treatment is required, total cost can be very different.

# 1. Symptoms and breast changes

Breast changes that deserve medical assessment can include:
- a new lump;
- thickening;
- nipple changes/discharge;
- skin dimpling;
- change in breast shape;
- underarm lump;
- persistent focal symptoms.

Many breast symptoms have non-cancer causes. A symptom list cannot diagnose cancer.

An undiagnosed person should receive appropriate clinical evaluation rather than being funnelled directly into an international cancer-treatment purchase.

# 2. Diagnosis: biopsy is central

Imaging can identify suspicious findings, but pathology establishes the tissue diagnosis.

An international case may include:
- mammography;
- breast ultrasound;
- MRI in selected situations;
- core biopsy;
- pathology;
- lymph-node sampling;
- staging imaging where indicated.

Canopus should distinguish:
- “suspected breast cancer”;
- “pathology-confirmed breast cancer”;
- “stage known”;
- “biomarkers complete.”

That changes which hospital team can respond meaningfully.

# 3. Breast cancer staging

NCI says breast cancer stage is based on:
- tumour size/location;
- lymph-node spread;
- distant spread;
- tumour grade;
- biomarkers such as hormone receptors and HER2.

Clinical staging can be assigned before surgery; pathological staging can incorporate surgical tissue when surgery occurs first.

For comparison, every hospital response should state what stage/information it is assuming.

---

# 4. Biomarkers: why ER, PR and HER2 matter

A **receptor** is a protein on or inside a cell that can receive a biological signal. Breast-cancer cells are tested for receptors because the result can help the oncology team understand what may be driving the cancer and whether hormone-blocking or HER2-targeted medicines may be relevant. A positive result does not select a treatment by itself; it is interpreted with stage, grade, previous treatment and the rest of the pathology.

Breast cancer is routinely assessed for biological features that can influence treatment.

Important fields in the case packet often include:
- estrogen receptor (ER);
- progesterone receptor (PR);
- HER2;
- grade;
- other molecular/genetic results when clinically relevant.

NCI explains that HER2-positive, hormone-receptor-positive and other molecular features can determine eligibility for targeted/hormonal treatment.

Do not translate one biomarker result into an automated treatment recommendation.

# 6. Main breast cancer treatment components

## Breast-conserving surgery (lumpectomy)
NCI describes lumpectomy/breast-conserving surgery as removal of the tumour and nearby breast tissue. Radiation often forms part of breast-conserving treatment.

## Mastectomy
Removal of the breast, with procedure details varying. Reconstruction can be immediate, delayed or not performed depending on clinical and personal factors.

## Lymph-node surgery
Sentinel lymph-node biopsy and/or more extensive axillary surgery may be used depending on the case.

## Radiation
Can be part of local treatment after breast-conserving surgery and in selected post-mastectomy situations.

## Chemotherapy
Used in selected patients depending on stage, tumour biology and risk.

## Hormone therapy
Relevant when the cancer is hormone-receptor positive.

## Targeted therapy
NCI describes targeted therapies for selected molecular groups, including HER2-positive breast cancer and other biomarker-defined settings.

## Immunotherapy
NCI notes immunotherapy is used in selected triple-negative breast cancer contexts.

The treatment sequence is individualized.

# 7. Surgery first or treatment first?

This is a major comparison question.

NCI’s stage-based guidance says many early-stage breast cancers begin with surgery, while larger or locally advanced disease may begin with chemotherapy or targeted treatment before surgery.

Why treatment may occur before surgery can include:
- shrinking the tumour;
- assessing response;
- facilitating breast-conserving approaches in selected cases;
- treating biologically aggressive disease early.

Do not apply this explanation to one patient without oncology review.

# 8. Example treatment sequences

## Surgery-first
```text
diagnosis + staging
      ↓
breast/lymph-node surgery
      ↓
final pathology
      ↓
multidisciplinary review
      ↓
radiation and/or systemic treatment as appropriate
      ↓
surveillance / long-term therapy
```

## Neoadjuvant
```text
diagnosis + biomarkers
      ↓
systemic treatment
      ↓
response assessment
      ↓
surgery
      ↓
surgical pathology
      ↓
additional treatment decision
```

## Metastatic
```text
diagnosis + biomarkers + extent
      ↓
systemic-treatment strategy
      ↓
response / toxicity monitoring
      ↓
continue or modify
      ↓
local/palliative interventions when indicated
```

A quote should clearly identify which pathway it assumes.

# 10. Reconstruction: separate the clinical and cosmetic assumptions

Reconstruction may involve:
- implant-based approaches;
- autologous tissue;
- immediate or delayed reconstruction;
- staged procedures.

It affects:
- operation time;
- hospital stay;
- recovery;
- number of procedures;
- cost;
- radiation planning in some settings.

A “mastectomy price” without saying whether reconstruction is included is not comparable.

---

# 11. Chemotherapy and targeted treatment cycles

NCI explains chemotherapy may be delivered in cycles and that the regimen depends on cancer type, stage, treatment goal and other factors.

For breast cancer, a quote for systemic treatment should specify:
- regimen/drug names;
- dose assumptions if relevant;
- number of planned cycles/doses;
- schedule;
- supportive medication;
- blood-test monitoring;
- growth-factor support if applicable;
- expected response assessment;
- what happens if treatment changes.

Do not compare “one cycle” to “full protocol” as the same price unit.

---

# 12. Radiation planning

Questions:
- Why is radiation recommended?
- Breast/chest wall?
- Regional lymph nodes?
- Technique?
- Number of fractions?
- Simulation/planning date?
- Is treatment daily?
- Can the full course be completed in India?
- Can radiation be safely transferred to a home-country centre?

A radiation cost should state whether it covers planning plus the complete intended fraction course.

# 13. Treatment side effects and travel

Side effects depend on treatment and can include:
- post-operative pain/wound issues;
- fatigue;
- nausea;
- low blood counts;
- infection risk;
- neuropathy;
- skin changes with radiation;
- specific drug-related effects.

The international plan should identify:
- emergency contact;
- home-country oncologist;
- where blood tests happen;
- who manages treatment toxicity;
- whether air travel is advisable at each point.

# 15. How to shortlist an Indian breast cancer centre

Do not begin with a generic “top 10.” A breast-cancer case may need breast or surgical oncology, medical oncology, radiation oncology, specialist pathology and reconstruction input rather than one doctor in isolation.

Ask each hospital to state which branch will review the case, which specialties will be involved and whether a named consultant has actually accepted the record review. Verify the assigned clinician's current role on the hospital's official directory immediately before you make a decision; directory presence alone is not an endorsement or a treatment plan.

# 27. FAQ

## How much does breast cancer treatment cost in India?
There is no one total. Apollo currently publishes about ₹1–2.5 lakh for breast cancer surgery, but complete treatment may also include pathology, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, radiation, reconstruction and follow-up.

## Lumpectomy or mastectomy?
The correct surgery depends on disease and patient factors. NCI describes both as standard surgical approaches in appropriate cases.

## Does every patient need chemotherapy?
No. Treatment depends on stage, tumour biology and other factors.

They help define tumour biology and can influence systemic treatment choices.

## Can I get surgery in India and chemotherapy at home?
Sometimes care can be coordinated across countries, but the oncology teams must agree on the plan, drug availability and monitoring.

# 33. Care across two countries

Some international breast-cancer journeys are hybrid.

Possible pattern:
- pathology/second opinion in India;
- surgery in India;
- chemotherapy/radiation at home.

Or:
- neoadjuvant therapy at home;
- surgery in India;
- ongoing therapy back home.

Questions that must be resolved:
- Does the home team agree?
- Are the same medicines available?
- Who owns dose changes?
- Can radiation planning be transferred safely?
- What records are required?
- Who manages emergencies?
- Where are response scans performed?
- Who reviews them?
- How quickly can teams communicate?
