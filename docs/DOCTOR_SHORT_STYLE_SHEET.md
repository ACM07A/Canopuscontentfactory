# Doctor-style short video system

## What we are making

The presenter is a clearly fictional AI medical educator. The screen must say this in the opening or closing frame. It is not a real doctor, does not diagnose, and does not impersonate a named clinician. Canopus Care remains a facilitator, not a provider.

## Format reverse-engineering

Public doctor-short guidance consistently converges on the same useful mechanics: name the exact patient question in the first two seconds, stay on one message, use a direct-to-camera authority cue, introduce a re-hook or contrast before the payoff, present three memorable beats, then end with a save/watch-next/soft enquiry CTA. Generic openings and long introductions spend the retention window before the topic is clear. The pattern is adapted here as a safety-first system, not copied from any creator.

## Yapper / VSL reference adaptation

The supplied X reference links to a health-and-wellness UGC swipe file labelled “Yapper VSL-style ad.” Its repeatable mechanics are:

- Blunt curiosity-led hook text appears immediately, often as large top text.
- Close direct-to-camera delivery in a lived-in room or clinic-like setting, with small hand movements and jump-cut changes instead of a polished commercial monologue.
- Yellow, high-contrast captions sit in the lower safe area and emphasize the spoken phrase.
- A prop or visual proof cue appears early: a blank checklist, records folder, scan illustration or travel document—not private patient data.
- The narrative expands from one problem to three reasons/questions, then resolves with a low-pressure action.

For Canopus Care, “proof” means a documented coordination process or source-backed information. It never means a cure, outcome promise, fake review, fake credential or invented patient story. The presenter is labelled `FICTIONAL AI EDUCATOR` and is not a real clinician.

## 25-second narrative template

1. 0–3s — Hook: “Before you compare hospitals for knee replacement abroad, ask these three questions.” Presenter looks at camera; large question on screen.
2. 3–6s — Re-hook: “The headline price is not the whole decision.” Presenter turns to a three-item board.
3. 6–11s — Beat 1: records the hospital needs to review.
4. 11–16s — Beat 2: inclusions, exclusions and validity of a written estimate.
5. 16–21s — Beat 3: who handles follow-up after the patient returns home.
6. 21–25s — CTA/disclosure: request a documented coordination pathway; general education; clinical decisions stay with the treating team.

## Visual rules

- Same fictional doctor reference image across the whole short; no accidental replacement with a stock patient, caregiver or unrelated presenter.
- Doctor visible in every beat; use restrained crop changes and information cards rather than random b-roll.
- White coat is a visual costume only; never add a name, specialty, hospital, credential or years-of-experience claim.
- 9:16, 1080×1920, large safe-margin typography, one message per frame, high contrast.
- Palette: Canopus green `#126342`, ink `#14251f`, care mint `#e6f4ec`, warm paper `#f7faf8`.
- Captions match the spoken words. No tiny legal copy. Disclosure is spoken or readable in the final frame.
- UGC adaptation: medium close-up, mild handheld feel, restrained jump cuts, natural pauses and a real-room background; avoid glossy AI-commercial lighting and frozen presenter poses.
- Use yellow captions with a dark outline and two lines maximum. Keep the first hook as a separate large text card, then switch to phrase-sized captions.
- Use model-generated presenter footage for roughly two-thirds of the runtime. Use licensed Freepik cutaways for roughly one-third: scans, records, hospital corridors, planning documents or destination logistics. Never use unrelated stock people as the doctor.

## Sound rules

- Use a human-approved English voice, local TTS only for preview, or an explicitly configured provider voice.
- Speech is the lead; music is optional and must sit below it. No robotic “announcement” cadence.
- Keep the first sentence short, pause after each numbered question, and leave 0.5–1 second of air before the CTA.

## QA before release

- Presenter identity matches the reference image in every scene.
- No lip-sync or hand/face artefacts that make the presenter look broken.
- Hook is understandable with sound off and within the first frame.
- The spoken copy contains no diagnosis, cure, outcome promise, price or invented credential.
- Captions, voiceover, title, description, disclosure, CTA and UTM package agree.
- A human reviewer approves the final package before paid use or publishing.
