# Canopus Care patient-guide editorial standard

**Updated:** 14 August 2026

These pages are written for patients and caregivers, not for search engines or internal content operations. Search visibility is a consequence of useful, original work; it is not the voice of the article.

## Voice

- Begin with the decision a patient or caregiver is actually facing.
- Use concrete daily-life details only when they clarify preparation, recovery or continuity of care.
- Vary structure by specialty and treatment journey. Do not force every treatment into identical headings or paragraph rhythms.
- Explain uncertainty directly. Never convert an indicative response into certainty.
- Keep the facilitator boundary clear without repeating the same disclaimer in every section.

## Evidence

- Put authoritative sources near the clinical orientation they support, not only in a long bibliography.
- Prefer government bodies, clinical societies and official patient guidance.
- Treat hospital pages as attributed operational or price signals, not independent evidence of superiority or outcomes.
- Recheck volatile prices, visa rules, accreditations, doctors and hospital programs immediately before publication.
- A medical reviewer must approve clinical claims before a page is indexed publicly.

## Human usefulness

Every treatment guide should answer four different needs:

1. What the treatment or diagnosis discussion actually means.
2. Which records and unresolved questions change the plan.
3. What the patient and caregiver will have to manage in daily life.
4. How care continues after the patient leaves India.

## Images

- Never present stock or generated people as real Canopus Care patients.
- Use consented patient stories only with a signed image and medical-detail release.
- Caption editorial photography as illustrative and preserve photographer/source attribution.
- Representation should reflect the intended international audience without stereotypes, tokenism or assumptions about a person's diagnosis.

## Search-quality guardrails

- No customer-facing labels such as “search intent,” “competitor-level guide,” “localisation status,” “content brief” or “conversion intent.”
- No doorway pages that differ only by location or keyword.
- No unsupported “best,” “top,” “success rate,” “lowest price” or guaranteed-outcome language.
- Do not optimize for an imagined AI detector. Optimize for originality, source quality, patient comprehension, clear authorship and medical review.
